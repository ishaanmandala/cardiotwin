CardioTwin v0.1

What would a heart scan show?

CardioTwin reads a 10-second ECG (the heart's electrical signal) and builds a digital twin of the heart: an estimate of what an echocardiogram, an ultrasound scan of the heart, would see.

    The 10-second ECG

    How to read an ECG in 30 seconds

    • P wave – the small bump: the upper chambers squeeze.
    • QRS – the tall spike: the main pumping chambers squeeze. Its height and width carry clues about muscle thickness and size.
    • T wave – the rounded bump: the main chambers recharge for the next beat.

    How CardioTwin works

    1. 1
      Record

      A normal 10-second ECG: stickers on the skin pick up the heart's electrical signal.

    2. 2
      Clean

      The signal is tidied up: noise from muscles, breathing and power lines is filtered out.

    3. 3
      Read

      Five neural networks, trained on hundreds of real people who had both an ECG and a heart scan, each make an estimate. Their average is the answer; how much they disagree tells us how sure to be.

    4. 4
      Build the twin

      The estimates are compared with healthy ranges and drawn as a heart you can explore, plus a forecast for the next 4 years.

    Good to know

    • This is a research project, not a medical test. Only a real echocardiogram can measure the heart.
    • The AI learned from adults with heart failure in Spain. For people with healthy hearts, children or other ECG machines, its estimates may be off, usually looking worse than reality.
    • Every number comes with a likely range. A wide range means the ECG didn't pin the answer down.
    • Worried about your heart? Talk to a doctor – not an AI.
